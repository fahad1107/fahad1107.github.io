What Is eBay Dropshipping — and Why Germany?

eBay dropshipping is a fulfilment model where you list products on eBay without purchasing them in advance. When a buyer places an order, you purchase the item from a supplier at a lower price and have it shipped directly to the customer. The difference between what the buyer pays on eBay and what you pay the supplier is your profit margin.

No warehouse. No upfront stock purchase. No packing tape. It sounds almost too simple — and in some ways it is, which is why so many people rush into it and run into problems. The business model itself is completely legitimate. The issues come from how it's executed.

Germany makes it particularly compelling. eBay.de is one of the most active eCommerce marketplaces in Europe, with millions of active buyers across virtually every product category. German consumers are known for reliable payment, high average order values, and consistent purchasing behaviour. For a dropshipping business that relies on volume and repeat transactions, that's a powerful foundation.

💡
Key fact: Germany is consistently among the top 3 largest eCommerce markets in Europe. eBay.de alone generates billions in annual sales. The opportunity is real — but so is the legal framework around it.

Is eBay Dropshipping Legal in Germany?

Yes — eBay dropshipping is entirely legal in Germany. The German legal system does not prohibit the dropshipping model itself. What German law does require is that you operate your business correctly: register it properly, handle VAT accurately, issue compliant invoices, and follow eBay's marketplace rules.

The confusion around legality usually comes from one of three places:

  • People hear about eBay account suspensions and assume the business is banned — it's not, the account was suspended for policy violations, not the model itself
  • People hear about Finanzamt (tax office) issues and assume the business is risky — it is, if you ignore your VAT obligations
  • Competitors spread misinformation to thin out the market

The truth is straightforward: run it as a proper German business, comply with eBay's seller policies, and eBay dropshipping is a fully legitimate online business that thousands of people operate successfully in Germany right now.

✅
Bottom line: eBay dropshipping is legal in Germany. German commercial law does not prohibit the dropshipping fulfilment model. The requirement is that you operate it as a legitimate, registered, VAT-compliant business.

The 5 Legal Requirements You Must Meet

Here are the five areas where German law and platform rules intersect. Getting all five right means you can operate with complete peace of mind. Missing even one can create serious problems down the line.

1

Register a Gewerbe (Business Registration)

If you're selling on eBay commercially — meaning regularly and with the aim of making profit — you are legally required to register a Gewerbe at your local Gewerbeamt. This is simple, fast, and costs between €20–€60. Without it, you're operating illegally as an unregistered business in Germany.

2

Apply for a USt-IdNr (VAT ID Number)

Once your Gewerbe is registered, you apply for a Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer (USt-IdNr) through the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern. This is your German VAT identification number. It's free, it's required for B2B transactions and cross-border sales, and it needs to appear on all your invoices.

3

Collect and Remit 19% VAT

Germany's standard VAT rate is 19%. You must charge VAT on your eBay sales, file regular advance VAT returns (Umsatzsteuervoranmeldung) with your Finanzamt, and remit the collected VAT on schedule. Getting this wrong is the most common and most expensive mistake new sellers make.

4

Issue VAT-Compliant Invoices

Every eBay transaction that generates a tax invoice must include your name, address, USt-IdNr, the buyer's details, a unique invoice number, itemised description, net amount, VAT rate, and VAT amount. Accounting software like Lexware Office makes this straightforward and is the recommended tool for German eBay sellers.

5

Maintain Compliant Bookkeeping (SKR04)

German law requires you to maintain orderly financial records. For small businesses, the Einnahmen-Überschuss-Rechnung (EÜR — income/surplus statement) is usually sufficient. The SKR04 chart of accounts is the standard for service businesses and is supported by Lexware Office. Records must be kept for 10 years.

eBay's Own Rules — What Most People Miss

Here's something many guides don't tell you: German law says dropshipping is fine, but eBay has its own layer of rules — and violating those rules will get your account suspended regardless of how legally compliant your business is. Account suspensions are the main operational risk for eBay dropshipping businesses, not legal issues with German law itself.

What eBay Allows

eBay explicitly allows dropshipping — as long as you are the seller of record and take full responsibility for the sale. This means you're the one answerable to the buyer, regardless of where the product ships from. eBay's dropshipping policy, as stated in their own documentation, permits the model when the seller guarantees delivery and handles customer issues.

What eBay Does Not Allow

  • Shipping directly from another retailer to the customer — buying from Amazon, Zalando, or similar retail platforms and dropshipping to your eBay buyer is against eBay's policy. Retail arbitrage of this type is a bannable offense.
  • Fake tracking numbers — uploading tracking numbers that don't correspond to real shipments, or using placeholder numbers to artificially close orders, is a serious policy violation that leads to permanent suspension.
  • Inconsistent delivery windows — promising 2–3 day delivery when your supplier takes 10+ days is a fast track to negative feedback, defect rates, and account restrictions.
  • Duplicate listings — listing the same item from multiple accounts or using tactics that game eBay's search algorithm.
⚠️
The real risk: The number one way eBay dropshipping businesses fail in Germany is not through legal problems — it's through eBay policy violations. Account health metrics, defect rates, and late shipment rates all feed into eBay's seller standards. Falling below these thresholds results in restrictions or account suspension. This is why working with someone who knows eBay.de's specific rules matters.

German VAT for eBay Sellers — Simplified

German VAT — called Mehrwertsteuer (MwSt.) or Umsatzsteuer (USt.) — is the biggest administrative hurdle for new eBay sellers. It's also the area where the most costly mistakes happen. Here's what you actually need to know, without the legal jargon.

The Kleinunternehmerregelung — Small Business Exemption

If your annual turnover is below €25,000 in the previous year and below €100,000 in the current year (updated thresholds from 2025), you can apply for the Kleinunternehmerregelung under §19 UStG. This exempts you from charging VAT on your invoices and from filing advance VAT returns. However, it also means you cannot reclaim input VAT from business expenses. For most growing eBay businesses, this exemption quickly becomes a limitation — and if you exceed the threshold mid-year, you must switch to the standard VAT regime immediately.

Standard VAT Regime (Regelbesteuerung)

Under the standard regime, you charge 19% VAT on all applicable sales, submit regular Umsatzsteuervoranmeldungen (monthly or quarterly, depending on your Finanzamt's requirements), and remit the collected VAT minus any VAT you paid on business inputs (Vorsteuer). You keep the margin — not the VAT.

7 Beginner Mistakes That Create Legal and Account Risk

These aren't theoretical risks — they're the actual mistakes Fahad sees again and again from new eBay sellers in Germany. Most are entirely avoidable with the right setup from day one.

🚫 1. No Gewerbe Registration

Selling on eBay regularly without registering a Gewerbe is illegal in Germany. The Finanzamt and local authorities do monitor this. Fines and back-tax demands can follow.

🚫 2. Ignoring VAT Obligations

Collecting money on eBay sales without reporting and remitting VAT creates tax debt that compounds with penalties. The Finanzamt can and does pursue this retroactively.

🚫 3. Retail Arbitrage (Amazon → eBay)

Buying from Amazon, Zalando, or similar platforms and dropshipping to eBay buyers violates eBay's dropshipping policy explicitly and leads to account suspension.

🚫 4. Fake Tracking Numbers

Uploading non-functional or placeholder tracking to close orders is a fast track to permanent account suspension and potential platform fraud classification.

🚫 5. Unrealistic Delivery Promises

Listing "2-3 day shipping" when your supplier ships in 10–14 days destroys your defect rate and late shipment rate, quickly pushing you below eBay's seller standards.

🚫 6. Missing or Wrong Invoices

German law requires VAT-compliant invoices. Missing your USt-IdNr, incorrect VAT amounts, or no invoice at all puts you in violation of §14 UStG and creates audit risk.

🚫 7. No Automation — Manual Chaos

Trying to manage a growing eBay dropshipping operation manually leads to errors, missed orders, and customer service disasters. Without automation tools and a reliable VPS, scaling becomes impossible.

🚫 8. Choosing Cheap Over Compliant

Using unregulated "cheap" eBay management services that use policy-violating methods gives short-term results and long-term account bans. A permanent suspension cannot be reversed.

How to Start an eBay Dropshipping Business in Germany — Step by Step

Here's the correct sequence. Following this order avoids the common mistake of going live before your compliance infrastructure is in place.

1

Register Your Gewerbe

Visit your local Gewerbeamt (or do it online in some cities). Cost: €20–€60. You'll receive a Gewerbeanmeldung confirmation within a few days. Keep this document — you'll need it for the next steps.

2

Get Your USt-IdNr (VAT ID)

Apply via the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern portal. It's free and takes 2–4 weeks. Until you receive it, keep your Finanzamt Steuernummer (tax number from your Finanzamt) handy as an interim identifier.

3

Set Up eBay.de Business Account

Create or convert your eBay account to a Business account. Add your USt-IdNr and complete your business details. A properly configured eBay business account is the foundation for compliant selling.

4

Set Up Accounting Software

Lexware Office with SKR04 is the recommended setup for German eBay sellers. Configure it to automatically generate compliant invoices for every eBay transaction and track your VAT position in real time.

5

Source Your Suppliers

Identify reliable wholesale or dropshipping suppliers — not retail platforms. Your supplier must be able to ship in acceptable timeframes, provide accurate tracking, and not include their own branding in packages.

6

Create Optimised eBay Listings

German-language, SEO-optimised titles and descriptions. Accurate category selection. Realistic, conservative shipping timeframes. High-quality product images. Getting listings right from the start builds your seller metrics faster.

7

Set Up VPS & Automation

Once orders start coming in, set up your Windows VPS (Frankfurt/German IP), install your repricing and order management tools, and configure monitoring. This is when you shift from manual to scalable.
