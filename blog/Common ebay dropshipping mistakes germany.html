Mistake 1 — Retail Arbitrage (Buying from Amazon to Sell on eBay)

This is the single biggest mistake in German eBay dropshipping in 2026, and it ends careers faster than any other. Retail arbitrage — buying from Amazon, Zalando, or Otto and shipping directly to eBay buyers — is explicitly prohibited by eBay's Dropshipping Policy. It is not a grey area. It is a stated violation.

Why do so many sellers still do it? Because it looks easy and the entry barrier is zero. No supplier research, no agreement negotiation, no minimum orders. Just copy an Amazon listing to eBay, mark it up, and wait for orders. The problem is that eBay's detection system in 2026 is sophisticated enough to identify this pattern within weeks.

The detection mechanisms are multiple: Amazon tracking numbers are cross-referenced against known logistics networks. Buyers report receiving Amazon-branded packages. eBay's algorithm notices price correlation between your eBay prices and Amazon's dynamic pricing. And if eBay ever requests supplier documentation — which they do routinely — a consumer Amazon receipt does not qualify as a wholesale supplier relationship.

🚫
The Fix: Source exclusively from wholesale suppliers with signed dropshipping agreements and proper B2B invoicing. See our Supplier Guide for Germany for exactly how to find and vet compliant suppliers.

Mistake 2 — Operating Without a Gewerbe Registration

German commercial law requires a Gewerbeanmeldung before your first commercial sale. Full stop. There is no minimum revenue threshold for this requirement — if you are selling regularly with the intent to profit, you are legally required to register.

The mistake sellers make is thinking they can delay registration until the business grows. This logic backfires catastrophically in Germany because:

  • The Finanzamt can demand back taxes plus 6% annual interest on all unregistered commercial income retroactively
  • Fines for Gewerbe violations range from €1,000 to €10,000
  • Since DAC7 launched, eBay automatically reports your sales data — the Finanzamt will know
  • Without a Gewerbe, you cannot get a USt-IdNr., open a proper business bank account, or get the supplier invoicing structure eBay requires

The cost to fix this is €20–65. The risk of not fixing it is potentially tens of thousands in penalties. Register your Gewerbe at your local Gewerbeamt before your first eBay sale. It takes one afternoon.

✅
The Fix: Register a Nebengewerbe (secondary business alongside your main job) or Hauptgewerbe. Read our complete Gewerbe Guide for eBay Germany for step-by-step registration instructions.

Mistake 3 — Ignoring the DAC7 Reporting Rule

Since January 2023 and fully enforced in 2026, the Platform Tax Transparency Act (DAC7) requires eBay to automatically report your seller data to the German Finanzamt when you exceed 30 transactions or €2,000 in annual sales. This is not optional, not avoidable, and not limited by your account status.

Many sellers — particularly those who started without a Gewerbe or who are not declaring eBay income — learn about DAC7 when they receive a Finanzamt letter. By that point, they face an investigation covering multiple years of undeclared income, with interest and penalties applied retroactively.

The psychological mistake is thinking that small-scale selling is below the radar. In Germany, there is no longer a radar to be below. DAC7 has automated the reporting. Every sale you make above the threshold is visible to the Finanzamt whether you declare it or not.

⚠️
The Fix: If you are already selling without proper registration, use the Selbstanzeige (voluntary disclosure) process immediately — it significantly reduces penalties when you come forward before the Finanzamt contacts you. Consult a Steuerberater immediately if this applies to you. Then register your Gewerbe and begin proper bookkeeping with Lexware Office (SKR04).

Mistake 4 — Getting VAT Calculations Wrong and Destroying Margins

This mistake does not cause account suspension — it causes businesses to appear profitable when they are actually losing money. There are two specific VAT errors that repeatedly appear in German eBay businesses:

Error 4a: Not accounting for 19% MwSt on eBay's fee

eBay Germany charges 19% MwSt on top of the final value fee for business sellers. This means if your FVF is €11 (11% of a €100 sale), you actually pay €13.09 in total eBay fees. Sellers who calculate margins based on the raw 11% are already 2% margin worse than they think before a single order ships.

Error 4b: Missing the Kleinunternehmerregelung threshold

The Kleinunternehmerregelung exempts sellers under €25,000 annual turnover from charging VAT. When you cross €100,000 in the current year, you must switch to the standard VAT regime immediately — not at year-end. Sellers who miss this cross it without noticing and create months of back-VAT liability.

Mistake 5 — Overpromising Delivery Times

This mistake is subtle but devastating to account health. German eBay buyers have high expectations — they are used to Amazon Prime and domestic retailers. When sellers list a 2–3 day delivery window but their Asian supplier ships in 12–20 days, every order becomes a defect.

eBay Germany's seller standards track Late Shipment Rate. Above 10% puts you Below Standard. At 15%+ for extended periods, account restrictions follow. The issue is compounding: late shipments generate negative feedback and Item Not Received cases, each of which adds a defect to your record independently.

The practical error is sellers copying delivery window promises from competitors without checking whether those windows are achievable with their actual suppliers. Always add 3–5 buffer days beyond your supplier's confirmed maximum delivery time. A buyer who receives their order one day early is happy. A buyer who receives their order one day late after you promised delivery has grounds for a case.

✅
The Fix: Always list delivery windows that are 3–5 days longer than your supplier's worst-case delivery time. Test 5 orders with each new supplier before scaling. Monitor your Late Shipment Rate in Seller Hub weekly — not monthly.

Mistake 6 — Listing Without a Supplier Agreement

Many dropshippers start listing on eBay Germany before establishing formal supplier relationships. They find products on a supplier's B2C website, order for customers as they come in, and consider the relationship established. It is not.

When eBay requests supplier documentation — which they do during account reviews, after high defect periods, and after buyer complaint clusters — you need to produce a signed dropshipping agreement and B2B invoices. Consumer purchase receipts from a supplier's B2C website do not qualify. An email chain with a supplier does not qualify. A signed agreement with proper B2B invoicing is what qualifies.

Without documentation, eBay can and does suspend accounts for failing to prove compliance with the Dropshipping Policy — even if you have been operating cleanly and the supplier relationship is genuine. Documentation is everything in a policy dispute.

📄
The Fix: Get a signed Dropshipping Agreement from every supplier before placing your first eBay order through them. The agreement should include both business names, VAT numbers, pricing terms, shipping terms, neutral packaging confirmation, and return handling. Mistake 7 — Running Multiple Products from a Single Account Without Risk Management

This is the scaling mistake. Sellers who have one successful product start adding more and more — different categories, different suppliers, different delivery windows — all on one eBay account. When one supplier starts failing (late shipments, out-of-stock, quality issues), it drags the entire account's metrics down.

A supplier quality problem in one product line can push your Late Shipment Rate or Defect Rate Above Standard across the entire account, affecting your rankings and visibility for all your other products that are performing perfectly. Everything is connected through account health.

Professional eBay Germany operations manage supplier performance actively. They have weekly supplier check-ins, they monitor delivery performance per supplier, and they pause listings when a supplier shows signs of reliability issues — before the defects accumulate.

✅
The Fix: Monitor your Seller Hub performance metrics weekly. Set alerts when Late Shipment Rate exceeds 5% (well before the 10% threshold). Have backup suppliers for your top-performing products. Pause listings immediately when a supplier shows delivery problems — it is cheaper to lose a few sales than to damage your account health.
